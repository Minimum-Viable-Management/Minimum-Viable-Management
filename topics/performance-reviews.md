# 🧾 Performance Reviews

Performance reviews work best as a summary of feedback and growth already discussed.
Mike and Neha focus on making reviews predictable, balanced, and useful for future development.

## 🔑 Key Points

- Treat reviews as a summary of feedback you have already shared and a checkpoint on career direction.
  Surprises damage trust, so the final rating should feel predictable.

- Document early signals even when you only have one data point.
  Update the record when behaviour changes so temporary issues do not become permanent labels.

- Balance praise with clear improvement areas so high performers keep growing and low performers are not blindsided.
  Honest, specific feedback beats vague positivity.

- Build feedback into the project lifecycle by agreeing goals, checking in at milestones, and debriefing at the end.
  Managers of managers can add mid-cycle rating and surprise checks to trigger earlier conversations.

## 🛠️ Put It Into Practice

1. At the start of each project, agree on goals, stretch areas, and the feedback your report wants you to collect.
2. Hold milestone and end-of-project debriefs so feedback is frequent and the formal review becomes a summary.
3. Run a mid-cycle rating and surprise check for every report and use gaps to schedule direct conversations.
4. Write the review around three prompts: proud stretch wins, what did not go to plan, and the next risk to take.
